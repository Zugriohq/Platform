# Deriv synthetic families: how each one behaves, and what could pay (2026-10-14, `UNVALIDATED_RESEARCH`)

**The owner's point (2026-10-14).** Each Deriv family behaves differently, and Deriv's own description of each says how. TTI partly categorised them. One engine across all of them will not find an edge.

So this catalogue covers every family on Deriv's page. For each one it records:
- what Deriv says it does;
- what TTI did with it;
- what that behaviour could pay, if anything;
- the rule the Market Lab family study tests (`behaviour_prereg.json`, registered before any result).

**Source.** All descriptions are from Deriv's page, deriv.com/markets/derived-indices/synthetic-indices, read 2026-10-14. Deriv says all of these indices are:
- "generated by a cryptographically secure random number generator";
- unaffected by news;
- such that "any noticeable historical patterns are purely coincidental".

It makes one exception: for **Range Break**, "channel analysis and indicators may be effective".

## What TTI 4.2 knew about the families

TTI's per-asset knowledge is one function, `assetIntel`. It covers five families with one signal each. That signal adds 0.2–0.35 to TTI's confluence score.

| TTI tag | Families | Signal | What it assumes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `streak` | Step | 5–7 candles closing the same way | that runs continue: false if steps are independent coin flips |
| `spike` | Boom 500/1000, Crash 500/1000 | "Pre-spike" and "Overdue": bars since the last big bar beyond 40% / 70% of 400 or 800 | that a spike becomes *due*: false if spikes arrive memorylessly ("on average" every N ticks says nothing either way) |
| `cons` | Range Break 100/200 | 4–8 narrow bars ("RB tighten", "RB cons") | that a squeeze precedes the break |
| `jump` | Jump 10–100 | a bar over 3 ATR in the last five | that a jump tells you something about what follows |
| `mo` | Boom (BUY), Crash (SELL) | a fixed trade direction | that the spike side is the side to trade |

Beyond `assetIntel`, TTI's families differ in only two other places:
- scalp versus day-trade timing (Volatility 1s, Step and Boom/Crash scalp);
- the entry-zone tolerance.

TTI has no notion of the following families: DEX, Drift Switch, Volatility Switch, Multi Step, Skew Step, Trek, Hybrid and Daily Reset. Every one of TTI's family signals is now a pre-registered rule, so each assumption is tested rather than believed.

## What would count as a finding

The study's rules are fixed in `Behaviour.cs`. They are tested on generated paths with known answers, in `BehaviourTests`:
- they find drift regimes, a bouncing range, a spike that comes due, a sticky step and a declared drift;
- they find nothing on a random walk, memoryless spikes, fair steps or symmetric jumps.

So a "no edge" verdict from the study means the structure is absent or too small to pay the spread. It does not mean the tool was blind.

**A candidate needs:**
- t ≥ 3.5;
- at least 50 trades;
- a positive average in both halves, after the spread.

It then needs a fresh forward period with the rule unchanged. Only after that is an engine considered (ADR-0011).

**Expected, before any run:**
- Most families will show no candidate, because Deriv states the generator is fair.
- Range Break and Drift Switch are where a pass is most plausible, since their declared mechanism is structure rather than noise.
- Trek and Daily Reset could carry a drift, if their direction is in the mean.

## How to run it

Install the new `ZugrioMarketLab.algo` and start it on the demo account. The family study is on by default, and the tick replay is off by default.
- It reads 21 days of M1 per symbol, 15 symbols per run, mechanism families first and Volatility last.
- Each symbol prints `LAB FAMILY` (what Deriv says), `LAB MEASURED`, `LAB RULE` lines and a `LAB STUDY VERDICT`.
- The run ends with `LAB STUDY SUMMARY` and the number to start the next batch from.

## Results, first run (2026-10-14; Deriv feed, amendment 1)

**Data.** 90 days of M1 bars per symbol from Deriv's public feed (2026-07-16 to 2026-10-14). The registered window is the last 21 days; the 69 days before it are the confirmation window.

**Spread.** Deriv's published CFD "min spread" (`deriv_specs_2026-10-14.json`). Results are in:
- `study_results_mechanism_families.txt`;
- `study_results_dsi_target_spread.txt`.

**Fix on the first run.** The range fade traded closes *beyond* the range. That put the stop behind the entry, and Range Break 100 read −∞ R. The fix matches the registered wording ("in its top/bottom 10%", i.e. inside the range), with a test. No other rule changed.

**What decides Drift Switch.** The structure is real and large, so the question is the account's actual spread and fill.
- Market Lab's tick replay now runs the same rules on the account's own bid/ask ticks by default: `SYMBOL|DSI`, `Behaviour.MomentumOnTicks`, entry 0, 2 and 5 seconds after the signal.
- It prints the average and highest spread actually paid.
- **That run, not this one, can make Drift Switch a candidate.**

**Controls (2026-10-14, `study_results_boom_crash_volatility.txt`).**
- **Boom and Crash 50–1000:** no edge. TTI's overdue rule is at best t 1.2. Drift-follow on Boom/Crash 50 loses heavily (t −12 to −13).
- **Volatility 10–100 and (1s) 10–150:** no edge, |t| ≤ 2.5 everywhere.
- **Volatility 250 (1s): t 13.2 for the fade, gross only.** This is an artefact, not structure. On Deriv's feed this index trades near 0.3, quoted to 2 decimals, so one price step (0.01) is about 3% of the price. Minutes move by −0.01, 0 or +0.01, and the rounding creates the reversion (AC1 −0.30). No spread is published for it, and it is not a candidate.
- **Drift Switch is not quantised:** about 60 price steps per typical minute's move.

**Tick check of Drift Switch 10 (`dsi10_tick_check.txt`, `dsitick.mjs`).**
- **Data:** Deriv's feed keeps about 24 hours of ticks: 87,316 ticks at 1 per second.
- **Method:** the rules filled at the first tick after the signal minute, plus 0, 2 or 5 seconds, buying at mid + spread/2 and selling at mid − spread/2.
- **Result:** the same as on bars. At the min spread, +0.58 to +1.03R per trade (t 10–15), with delays up to 5 seconds costing about 0.02R. At 3× the min spread (0.66) with a 5-second delay, still +0.29 to +0.73R.
- **So the signal does not depend on bar timing.** The account's real spread is the remaining question.

**Tick check of Drift Switch 20 and 30 (`dsi20_tick_check.txt`, `dsi30_tick_check.txt`; 24 h, 1 tick per second).** Same method as for Drift Switch 10.

All three Drift Switch indices keep their edge when filled tick by tick after the signal. The account's actual spread is what remains to be measured.
