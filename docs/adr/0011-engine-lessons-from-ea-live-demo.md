# ADR-0011: Engine lessons from the EA's first demo week (trend context for reclaims, stop distance, scalp exits)

- **Status:** Proposed, 2026-10-09. The owner asked that lessons from the EA "also inform the way the engine performs if that is necessary". This ADR records which ones are candidates for decision-core and what evidence promotes them. Nothing in decision-core changes until it is accepted.
- **Date:** 2026-10-09
- **Decision owners:** Founder / Product Lead (to decide); independent reviewer (to review)
- **Related:** ADR-0003 (strategy and entry contracts), ADR-0008, ADR-0010, frozen Signal Authority Architecture v1.0.2 (F-1, F-3), PR #116

## Context

The EA traded the owner's Deriv demo for its first full day on 2026-10-09. The owner's history (18 closed trades, 05–09 Oct):
- 7 wins, 11 losses. Net −104.81 on about 10,000.
- Every loss was about 1R (95–124). The sizing and stop discipline held.
- One XAUUSD win of +500.76 carried most of the gross.

Two failure patterns showed in the charts the owner shared:

1. **Fading a clear trend.** All six XAUUSD trades were sells. Gold rose from about 4,070 to 4,200 on H1, with higher highs and higher lows, and the last three sells lost.
   - Continuation sells are blocked by the EA's trend filter in that structure. These sells were therefore reversal-reclaims, which the EA exempted from the filter by default.
   - At a trending high, every poke above the high followed by a close back below looks like a failed breakout. The stop sits just beyond the high, where the next poke goes.
2. **Stops inside the noise.** One XAUUSD sell of 2.75 lots implies a stop about 0.45 away. That happens when the entry price has already moved past the level toward the stop, which decision-core allows within the chase distance.
   - Sizing kept the money risk at 1%. But a stop that close is hit by ordinary noise and spread: the loss was 1.24R.

The owner also asked for frequent short scalps: trades of minutes, small wins, many per day. On real data the SCALP style found no setups in its first hours. The EA now reports why each hour had none.

## Decision (EA now; decision-core only by acceptance of this ADR)

These rules are implemented in the EA (PR #116) as **abort-only or tighter-exit** rules. Each is switchable and labelled `UNVALIDATED_RESEARCH`. decision-core is unchanged.

| # | Rule (EA, now) | Default | Candidate decision-core change |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | A reversal-reclaim is refused when it fades a clear context trend: a sell while the structure is UP, a buy while it is DOWN. It stays allowed in MIXED or UNKNOWN structure (ranges). | on | A context-structure predicate in the reclaim route's READY conditions. |
| 2 | Minimum stop distance: entry is refused if price is closer to the stop than `MinStopAtr` × ATR. The default equals the model's stop buffer, so price may not be past the level. | 0.3 ATR | `geometryCurrent` requires the close on the objective side of the level, not merely within chase distance. |
| 3 | SCALP take-profit at the nearer of the engine objective and 1R. The setup is refused if the objective leaves less than 1R. | 1R | A horizon-profile option for an R-based objective (SCALP), alongside the swing objective. |
| 4 | SCALP time stop: close after 30 minutes through the risk-reducing gate. | 30 min | Time-based invalidation as part of a horizon profile (a SCALP setup that has not worked in N bars is no longer the setup). |
| 5 | SCALP minimum runway 0.3 ATR, so the objective only needs room for the 1R target (rule 3 still requires it). | 0.3 ATR | Per-horizon runway in the horizon profile. |
| 6 | Results by style, setup type and market (count, win rate, average R, net, hold time), read from the broker's history every hour. | always | The evidence input for any of the above (ADR-0004 strategy evidence health). |

The spread filter (spread ≤ 25% of the stop) is also a candidate. decision-core prices no costs today.

## Promotion criteria (proposed)

A rule moves into decision-core only with:
- **Evidence:** at least 50 closed trades of the affected setup type with and without the rule, from the EA log or a replay of the same setups. The average R with the rule must be better by more than its standard error.
- **Change control:** a separate PR per rule, with decision-core tests and the frozen-spec impact stated. It must not be combined with an architecture change (CLAUDE.md).
- **Product:** the Windows product picks the change up only through its own gates (`CURRENT-GATE.md`). F-3 still applies: no threshold becomes capital-authoritative without a validated, versioned State Policy.

## What the evidence says about scalping synthetics (for the owner)

- **No pattern edge by design.** Deriv's Volatility indices are generated by a random number generator, so price structure carries no edge. Over many trades a structure strategy on them earns about zero minus costs. Frequency multiplies whatever the expectancy is, including a negative one.
- **Simulation.** A tick-consistent random walk was replayed through the EA's SCALP rules: 192 market-hours, 33 trades.
  - Results: about 0.17 trades per market per hour, held 2–7 minutes, 61% won, average +0.16R ± 0.17R.
  - That is statistically zero, as theory says.
  - A first replay with independent bar wicks showed a false edge (mean-reverting wicks that real ticks do not have), which is why the tick-consistent replay is the one recorded here.
- **What can produce an edge.** Only a market with real structure (forex, gold, and these synthetics only if their generator is not a pure random walk) can supply one, and only data shows whether it does. Rule 6 is the instrument for that.

## Consequences

- Fewer gold fades at trending highs, and fewer tiny-stop entries. Some valid reversals at real turns are skipped while the structure still reads UP or DOWN.
- Scalps exit faster and at 1R. Their win rate and expectancy are now visible per style.
- None of this is a performance claim. The sample is one week and 18 trades.

## Rollback

- Each EA rule has a setting:
  - *Never fade a clear trend (reclaim setups)*
  - *Minimum stop distance*
  - *Scalp: take profit at*
  - *Scalp: close after*
  - *Scalp: minimum runway*
- Setting a rule to off or 0 restores the previous behaviour. Reverting the PR #116 commit removes them all.
